Burns and scalds are damage to the skin caused by heat, chemicals, electricity, or radiation. Immediate and correct first aid can dramatically reduce pain and permanent tissue scarring.

## What to do immediately
1. Stop the burning process immediately by removing the person from the heat source or turning off electricity.
2. Cool the burn under clean, cool running tap water (12°C to 18°C) for at least 20 minutes.
3. Remove constrictive clothing, rings, and jewelry near the affected area before swelling begins. Do not remove anything that is stuck to the burn.
4. Keep the person warm using a blanket to prevent hypothermia, but do not touch the burned area.
5. Cover the burn loosely with clean cling film or a sterile, non-adherent dressing.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT apply ice or iced water; extreme cold damages delicate healing cells.
- Do NOT burst blisters; they act as a natural sterile biological barrier against infection.
- Do NOT apply butter, toothpaste, oil, or home remedies, as they trap heat and introduce bacteria.
- Do NOT touch the burn directly with bare hands or adhesive bandages.

## When to go to hospital
- All chemical and electrical burns.
- Large or deep burns bigger than the palm of the casualty's hand.
- Burns on the face, neck, hands, feet, joints, or genitals.
- Any burn on infants, young children, or elderly patients.

## Signs of serious burn (RED FLAGS)
- White, charred, or leathery skin that is numb to touch.
- Difficulty breathing or coughing up black soot (inhalation injury).
- Signs of shock: rapid shallow breathing, cold clammy skin, or dizziness.
