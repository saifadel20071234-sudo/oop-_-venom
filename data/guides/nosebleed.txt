Nosebleeds are common and usually benign, caused by the bursting of tiny fragile blood vessels in Kiesselbach's plexus in the anterior nasal septum.

## What to do immediately
1. Sit upright and lean slightly forward (do NOT tilt head back).
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose just below the nasal bone using thumb and index finger.
3. Maintain continuous pinching for 10 to 15 minutes without releasing to check.
4. Breathe through the mouth and spit out any blood that collects in the throat.
5. Apply a cold pack or ice wrapped in cloth over the nasal bridge and forehead.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT tilt head backwards; this causes blood to drain down the throat, causing vomiting or aspiration.
- Do NOT pack nostrils with tissue paper, cotton wool, or cloth.
- Do NOT blow your nose or pick it for several hours after bleeding stops.

## When to go to hospital
- Bleeding continues after 20 minutes of continuous firm pinching.
- Severe blood loss or difficulty breathing.
- Nosebleed occurs following a head injury or road traffic accident.

## Signs of serious epistaxis (RED FLAGS)
- Feeling faint, dizzy, or lightheaded.
- Blood running down the back of the throat even when pinching the front of the nose (posterior nosebleed).
