Animal bites carry high risks of severe bacterial infection and rabies. Venomous snake and scorpion stings can inject neurotoxic or hemotoxic venom.

## What to do immediately
For animal bites (Dogs, Cats):
1. Immediately wash the wound thoroughly with soap and warm running water for at least 15 minutes.
2. Apply an antiseptic solution (povidone-iodine).
3. Cover with a sterile dressing.
4. Seek medical care immediately for Rabies Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) vaccine and tetanus booster.

For snake and scorpion stings:
1. Keep the casualty calm and completely still; physical activity accelerates venom circulation.
2. Immobilize the bitten limb using a splint and keep it at or slightly below heart level.
3. Remove rings, bracelets, and restrictive shoes before swelling occurs.
4. Transport immediately to the nearest hospital stocked with polyvalent antivenom.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT apply a tight arterial tourniquet.
- Do NOT cut the wound, suck out venom with mouth, or apply ice or electric shocks.
- Do NOT attempt to catch or kill the snake.

## When to go to hospital
- All animal bites require professional evaluation for rabies vaccination.
- All snake and scorpion envenomations require immediate hospital admission.

## Signs of venomous envenomation (RED FLAGS)
- Rapid swelling and severe bruising spreading up the limb.
- Drooping eyelids (ptosis), difficulty speaking, or swallowing (neurotoxicity).
- Spontaneous bleeding from gums or puncture sites.
