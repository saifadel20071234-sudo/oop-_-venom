Hypoglycemia occurs when blood glucose drops below normal levels (< 70 mg/dL or 3.9 mmol/L), commonly affecting individuals with diabetes taking insulin.

## What to do immediately
If the person is conscious and able to swallow (The "Rule of 15"):
1. Give 15 to 20 grams of fast-acting simple carbohydrates:
   - Half a glass (120-150 ml) of fruit juice or regular soda (non-diet).
   - 3 to 4 glucose tablets or 3 teaspoons of sugar dissolved in water.
2. Wait 15 minutes and recheck blood sugar level if a glucometer is available.
3. If still below 70 mg/dL or symptoms persist, give another 15 grams of fast-acting sugar.
4. Once recovered, have them eat a longer-acting complex carbohydrate snack (bread, crackers, or meal).

If the person is unconscious or having seizures:
1. Place in Recovery Position.
2. Administer emergency Glucagon injection or nasal powder if trained and available.
3. Call 123 (Ambulance) immediately.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT give fluids, sweets, or insulin to an unconscious person (severe choking and aspiration risk).
- Do NOT administer insulin; insulin lowers blood sugar further and can be fatal.

## When to go to hospital
- Unconsciousness or seizures.
- Blood sugar remains low despite two rounds of fast-acting sugar.

## Signs of severe hypoglycemia (RED FLAGS)
- Disorientation, combative behavior, or confusion.
- Profuse sweating, trembling, and pale clammy skin.
- Loss of consciousness and coma.
