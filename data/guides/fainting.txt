Fainting (syncope) is a temporary loss of consciousness caused by a sudden decrease in blood flow to the brain. Recovery is usually rapid and complete.

## What to do immediately
1. Lay the person flat on their back on the floor.
2. Elevate their legs about 30 cm (12 inches) above heart level to restore blood flow to the head.
3. Loosen tight collars, ties, belts, or restrictive clothing.
4. Ensure fresh air circulation; fan the person and clear crowds.
5. Check breathing and airway; if breathing normally, keep them resting for 10-15 minutes.
6. Help them sit up slowly before attempting to stand.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT give them anything to eat or drink while they are drowsy or unconscious.
- Do NOT splash water on the person's face or slap them to revive them.
- Do NOT let them stand up immediately after regaining consciousness.

## When to go to hospital
- The person does not regain consciousness within 1 minute.
- Fainting occurs during exercise or while lying down.
- Accompanied by chest pain, palpitations, or shortness of breath.

## Signs of serious fainting (RED FLAGS)
- Sustained unconsciousness lasting longer than 60 seconds.
- Incontinence (loss of bowel or bladder control).
- Unequal pupils, slurred speech, or confusion after waking (suspected stroke).
