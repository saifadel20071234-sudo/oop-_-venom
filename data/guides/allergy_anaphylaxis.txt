Anaphylaxis is a severe, rapidly progressing, and life-threatening systemic allergic reaction involving airways and circulation. Common triggers include food (nuts, seafood), insect stings, and medications (penicillin).

## What to do immediately
1. Administer an Epinephrine Auto-Injector (EpiPen) immediately into the outer mid-thigh muscle.
2. Call 123 (Ambulance) right away.
3. Keep the person lying flat with legs elevated. If they have difficulty breathing, allow them to sit up slightly.
4. If symptoms do not improve within 5 to 15 minutes, administer a second EpiPen dose if available.
5. Remove or avoid the allergic trigger if possible.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT make the person stand up or walk; sudden standing can cause fatal circulatory collapse.
- Do NOT delay giving epinephrine; antihistamines and inhalers are secondary and do not treat anaphylaxis.

## When to go to hospital
- Every patient with anaphylaxis must go to an emergency department and be monitored for at least 4 to 6 hours due to biphasic reactions.

## Signs of anaphylaxis (RED FLAGS)
- Swelling of the tongue, lips, throat, and uvula.
- Severe wheezing, stridor, or inability to speak.
- Widespread itchy hives and skin flushing.
- Dizziness, hypotension, and collapse.
