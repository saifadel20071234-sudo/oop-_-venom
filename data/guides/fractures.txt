A fracture is a break or crack in a bone. A sprain is an injury to ligaments around a joint. Proper immobilization prevents further neurovascular damage.

## What to do immediately
1. Keep the injured limb as still as possible; do not attempt to straighten or realign it.
2. Support the limb using a splint, sling, or soft padding above and below the injured joint.
3. Apply a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 15-20 minutes to reduce swelling.
4. Elevate the injured extremity if it does not increase pain.
5. If bone pokes through the skin (open fracture), cover with a clean sterile dressing without pressing.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT move the casualty if a spinal, neck, or pelvic fracture is suspected unless in danger.
- Do NOT apply ice directly to the bare skin.
- Do NOT allow the patient to bear weight or walk on an injured lower limb.

## When to go to hospital
- All suspected fractures require X-ray evaluation and orthopedic casting.
- Compound open fractures with bone protrusion or heavy bleeding.

## Signs of severe fracture (RED FLAGS)
- Obvious limb deformity or bone protruding through skin.
- Pale, cold, or bluish toes/fingers below the injury site with loss of pulse.
- Numbness, tingling, or inability to feel sensation in the fingers or toes.
