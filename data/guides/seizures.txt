A seizure is a sudden, uncontrolled electrical disturbance in the brain. It can cause changes in behavior, movements, feelings, and levels of consciousness.

## What to do immediately
1. Protect the person from injury: clear hard or sharp objects away from them.
2. Cushion their head with something soft (folded jacket or pillow).
3. Loosen tight neckwear or eyeglasses.
4. Time the duration of the seizure from start to finish.
5. When the shaking stops, gently roll the person onto their side into the Recovery Position to keep airway clear.
6. Stay with them until they are fully awake, alert, and oriented.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT hold the person down or try to restrain their movements.
- Do NOT put anything in their mouth (they cannot swallow their tongue; objects cause dental damage and airway obstruction).
- Do NOT offer food or drink until they are completely conscious and alert.

## When to go to hospital
- The seizure lasts longer than 5 minutes (status epilepticus).
- A second seizure occurs immediately without full recovery in between.
- The person is pregnant, injured, or has diabetes.
- This is the person's first known seizure.

## Signs of severe seizure emergency (RED FLAGS)
- Seizure lasting > 5 minutes.
- Difficulty breathing or remaining blue after convulsions stop.
- Failure to regain consciousness after 10-15 minutes.
