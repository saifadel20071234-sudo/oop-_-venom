Choking happens when an object or food lodges in the throat or windpipe (trachea), completely blocking the flow of air. Immediate recognition and intervention are life-saving.

## What to do immediately
For a conscious adult or child over 1 year:
1. Ask "Are you choking?" If they can cough forcefully or speak, encourage them to cough.
2. If they cannot speak, breathe, or make sound, give 5 back blows:
   - Stand behind them and lean them forward.
   - Deliver 5 firm blows between the shoulder blades with the heel of your hand.
3. If back blows fail, give 5 abdominal thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver):
   - Stand behind the person and wrap arms around their waist.
   - Make a fist with one hand and place it just above the navel.
   - Grasp your fist with the other hand and pull sharply inward and upward.
4. Repeat cycles of 5 back blows and 5 abdominal thrusts until the airway is clear.

For choking infants (under 1 year):
1. Lay the infant face down along your forearm, supporting their head and jaw with your fingers.
2. Deliver 5 quick back slaps between the shoulder blades.
3. Turn the infant face up and give 5 chest thrusts using 2 fingers in the center of the breastbone.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT perform abdominal thrusts on infants or pregnant women (use chest thrusts instead).
- Do NOT slap a person on the back while they are standing completely upright.
- Do NOT perform blind finger sweeps in the mouth; it may push the object deeper.

## When to go to hospital
- Anyone who required abdominal thrusts must be checked by a doctor for internal abdominal injuries.
- Persistent cough, painful swallowing, or hoarseness after choking.

## Signs of complete airway obstruction (RED FLAGS)
- Universal choking sign (hands clutching the throat).
- Inability to talk, cry, or cough.
- Skin, lips, and nail beds turning blue or grey (cyanosis).
- Loss of consciousness.
