An asthma attack occurs when the airways suddenly narrow, swell, and produce extra mucus, causing difficulty breathing.

## What to do immediately
1. Help the person sit upright comfortably. Do NOT allow them to lie flat.
2. Stay calm and reassure them; panic increases breathing effort.
3. Help them take their blue reliever inhaler (Salbutamol / Ventolin):
   - Give 1 puff every 30 to 60 seconds, up to 10 puffs, preferably using a spacer device.
4. If symptoms do not improve after 10 puffs, call 123 for an ambulance immediately.
5. Repeat 10 puffs every 10 to 15 minutes if the ambulance is delayed.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT make them lie down.
- Do NOT crowd around the casualty; ensure plenty of circulating fresh air.
- Do NOT give cold drinks or expose them to smoke or strong scents.

## When to go to hospital
- No improvement after 10 puffs of reliever inhaler.
- The person is too breathless to speak a full sentence in one breath.
- Peak flow reading below 50% of personal best.

## Signs of life-threatening asthma (RED FLAGS)
- Exhaustion, drowsiness, or confusion.
- Silent chest (no wheezing heard because airflow is too restricted).
- Cyanosis (bluish tint on lips, face, or fingernails).
