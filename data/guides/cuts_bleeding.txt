Bleeding occurs when blood vessels are damaged. Controlling severe external bleeding is one of the highest priorities in first aid to prevent hypovolemic shock.

## What to do immediately
1. Put on disposable nitrile gloves if available to protect against bloodborne infections.
2. Apply firm, direct pressure onto the wound using a sterile gauze pad, clean cloth, or clean hands.
3. Keep direct pressure steady and uninterrupted for at least 5 to 10 minutes.
4. Elevate the injured limb above heart level if no bone fracture is suspected.
5. If blood soaks through the dressing, do NOT remove it. Add more dressings on top and press firmly.
6. Once bleeding is controlled, secure the pad with a snug bandage.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT remove embedded objects (knife, glass) from the wound; pack dressings firmly around it.
- Do NOT remove the original blood-soaked gauze, as this disrupts the clotting process.
- Do NOT apply a tourniquet unless you are trained and direct pressure has failed in a catastrophic limb bleed.

## When to go to hospital
- The bleeding does not stop after 10-15 minutes of continuous firm pressure.
- The wound is deep, gaping, or exposes fat, tendon, or bone.
- The cut was caused by a dirty, rusty object or animal/human bite (tetanus and rabies risk).

## Signs of serious bleeding (RED FLAGS)
- Spurting, pulsatile bright red blood (arterial bleeding).
- Casualty becomes pale, confused, dizzy, or loses consciousness.
- Rapid weak pulse and cold clammy skin indicating shock.
