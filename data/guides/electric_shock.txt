Electric shock occurs when a person comes into contact with an electrical source. Electricity can cause severe thermal burns, deep internal muscle damage, and disrupt heart rhythms.

## What to do immediately
1. Ensure your own safety first: do NOT touch the casualty if they are still in contact with the live current.
2. Turn off the power source at the main circuit breaker or unplug the appliance.
3. If power cannot be switched off, use a dry, non-conductive object (wooden broom handle, dry cardboard, plastic rod) to push the wire away.
4. Once safe, check responsiveness and breathing:
   - If not breathing, call 123 and start CPR immediately.
5. Cool superficial entry and exit burn wounds with clean water.
6. Treat for shock and keep the casualty lying down and warm.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT touch the casualty with bare hands or conductive metal objects while current is live.
- Do NOT approach high-voltage power lines until authorities confirm power is grounded and disabled.

## When to go to hospital
- All victims of electric shock require hospital evaluation and an ECG to detect cardiac arrhythmias.

## Signs of severe electrical injury (RED FLAGS)
- Unresponsiveness, apnea, or cardiac arrest (ventricular fibrillation).
- Deep third-degree entry and exit burn wounds.
- Fractures or spinal injury from severe muscle contractions or falls.
