Sudden cardiac arrest occurs when the heart suddenly stops pumping blood. Immediate CPR and early defibrillation double or triple the chances of survival.

## What to do immediately
1. Verify scene safety and check responsiveness: tap shoulders and shout loudly "Are you OK?".
2. If unresponsive and not breathing normally (gasping or no breathing), yell for help: "Call 123 (Ambulance) and bring an AED!".
3. Place casualty on their back on a firm, flat surface.
4. Place the heel of one hand in the center of the chest (lower half of breastbone), and interlock your other hand on top.
5. Push hard and fast at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute, depressing the chest at least 5 to 6 cm (2 inches).
6. Allow complete chest recoil between compressions.
7. If trained in rescue breaths, give 2 breaths after every 30 compressions (30:2 ratio).
8. If untrained or unwilling to give breaths, deliver continuous Hands-Only CPR.
9. As soon as an Automated External Defibrillator (AED) arrives, turn it on and follow voice prompts.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT interrupt chest compressions for more than 10 seconds.
- Do NOT place your hands on the ribs or lower tip of the breastbone (xiphoid process).
- Do NOT hesitate to use an AED; it will only shock if a shockable rhythm is detected.

## When to go to hospital
- Immediate emergency ambulance transfer to a cardiac care center is essential for all cardiac arrests.

## Signs of cardiac arrest (RED FLAGS)
- Unresponsiveness (no reaction to shaking or shouting).
- Absence of normal breathing (agonal gasping).
- Absence of pulse (if checked by healthcare personnel).
