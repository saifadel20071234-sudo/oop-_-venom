Heat exhaustion is caused by loss of water and salt from excessive sweating. Heatstroke is a life-threatening medical emergency where the body's internal temperature regulation fails and core temperature exceeds 40°C.

## What to do immediately
For heat exhaustion:
1. Move the person immediately to a cool, shaded, or air-conditioned area.
2. Loosen or remove unnecessary outer clothing.
3. Have them sip cool water or an oral electrolyte rehydration solution.
4. Apply cool wet cloths to the skin or spray with water.

For heatstroke (Emergency):
1. Call 123 (Ambulance) immediately.
2. Rapidly cool the patient by any available means: ice baths, cool water sponge, or cold packs on neck, armpits, and groin.
3. Fan vigorously to promote evaporative cooling.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT give fluids by mouth to someone who is drowsy, confused, vomiting, or unconscious.
- Do NOT give aspirin or paracetamol; they do not reduce environmental hyperthermia and can harm kidneys.

## When to go to hospital
- Heat exhaustion that does not improve after 30 minutes of rest and cooling.
- Any suspected heatstroke case must be admitted to ICU immediately.

## Signs of heatstroke (RED FLAGS)
- Core body temperature above 40°C (104°F).
- Hot, dry, flushed skin (sweating has completely ceased).
- Confusion, delirium, seizures, or coma.
- Rapid pounding heart rate and rapid shallow breathing.
