Eye injuries can result from chemical splashes (acids, alkalis), blunt trauma, or foreign objects penetrating the eyeball.

## What to do immediately
For chemical splashes:
1. Immediately flush the eye with clean running water or sterile saline for at least 15 to 20 minutes continuously.
2. Hold the eyelid open and ensure water flows from the inner corner outwards to avoid contaminating the other eye.
3. Seek emergency ophthalmology care immediately.

For penetrating objects:
1. Protect the eye by covering it with a rigid shield (or the bottom of a paper cup taped around the eye).
2. Cover the unaffected eye as well to minimize consensual eye movement.
3. Keep the casualty calm and seated upright.

## Do NOT
- Do NOT rub the eye under any circumstances.
- Do NOT attempt to remove an impaled or embedded foreign object from the eyeball.
- Do NOT apply pressure or bandages tightly over the eye.

## When to go to hospital
- Any chemical splash in the eye.
- Penetrating injury, visible blood inside the cornea, or loss of vision.

## Signs of emergency eye trauma (RED FLAGS)
- Severe sudden loss or distortion of vision.
- Pupils unequal in size or irregular shape.
- Leaking fluid or protruding tissue from the eyeball.
